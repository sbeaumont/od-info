How OP and DP are calculated

OD Info never sees a dominion's offensive or defensive power. It rebuilds both from the ops you've taken, the same way you would by hand.

1. Which units get counted

Two ops report a dominion's army, and they tell you different things.

OpWhat it tells you
Clear Sight Every unit the dominion owns, one number per unit type. That includes units away on an invasion. It won't tell you how they're split, and it says nothing about the training queue.
Barracks Spy The split: how many are home, how many are training and land on which tick, how many are returning and land on which tick. Every number on it is fuzzed by up to {{ vm.barracks_spy_error_percent }}%.

The tool picks between them per dominion.

  • Clear Sight is fresher (or under an hour old): its totals get used, plus anything from the last Barracks Spy that hadn't finished training by the time the Clear Sight was taken. What they own now, plus what's still in the oven.
  • Barracks Spy is fresher: home, returning and training get added back together. Units at home are taken at the top of their fuzz range and returning units at the bottom, so the dominion reads as strong at home now.

The Paid column counts the ticks until the last of that training lands. Until then you're looking at OP they've paid for. Whether they can send it this tick is a different question.

That's the angle of the whole tool: the maximum a dominion could send once everything it has paid for has landed. It's the figure that fits the {{ vm.build_ticks }} tick planning window the rest of the app works in.

2. What one unit is worth

Each unit starts at its race's printed offense and defense. Then its own perks move the number.

The ones worth knowing about are the perks that depend on the dominion rather than the race, because each needs an extra op before the tool can read it.

Offense or defense from land
A Wood Elf Druid hits harder the more forest the dominion sits on. Needs a Land Spy. Without one the perk counts as zero.
Offense from wizard ratio
An Icekin Ice Elemental scales with raw WPA, read off Clear Sight.
Offense from prestige
An Orc Bone Breaker scales with prestige, also off Clear Sight.
Paired units
A Kobold Overlord is worth more when there are enough of the paired unit to go round. Counted straight from the army.

An exclamation mark next to Ops Age on the Military page means one of these applies to that dominion and the op it needs is missing. That unit is being counted at its printed strength.

3. The bonuses on top

Offense bonuses get added together first, then applied once to the raw OP, same as the game does it. Defense works the same way off its own list.

Anything the tool can't see counts as zero. So a dominion you have thin intel on always reads low, never high.

4. What each column means

Send OP / Send DP
A normal send: everything in the two offensive unit slots goes out, everything in the two defensive slots stays home. That's the realistic send for most races, and it's what you get until you switch the send type. A race whose third and fourth units are both hybrids has no such split, so those show the 5/4 figure here instead.
Fill in vs OP at the top of the page and the column changes meaning. It becomes the most OP that dominion could send while still holding off an attack of that size, spending its hybrids on defence first.
5/4 OP / 5/4 DP
The absolute maximum. Hybrids get thrown at the attack in order of how much offense they buy per point of defence given up, until sending one more would break the 5/4 rule. Treat it as the worst case rather than the likely one.
+Tmps
Send OP grossed up for that dominion's temples, so it's the number your DP actually has to beat. Compare against this column, not Send OP. It's also the one to sort on when you want the strongest dominion in the round with their temples already counted in.
Temples come off the multiplier on your defence, not off your finished DP, and that multiplier never drops below 1. So with M for your multiplier (1 plus your DP bonus) and t for their temple reduction, the column is Send OP × M ÷ max(M − t, 1). A bigger DP bonus dilutes their temples: the same 20% temples cost a defender sitting on walls, guard towers and Ares far less than one with nothing up.
That means the column is written from a defender's point of view, and by default that defender is you. The my DP bonus box at the top of the page starts at your own bonus as the ops have it. Type another number to read the round through a different defence — a realmie you're advising, or the bonus you'll have once your walls finish — and empty the box to go back to your own.
Raw OP / Raw DP
These cells show OP and DP with all bonuses applied. The genuinely raw figure is in the tooltip when you hover the cell.
Cur OP / Cur DP / Conf
Strength from home units only, worked out from Barracks Spies rather than Clear Sight, plus whatever has landed since. Two or more Barracks Spies taken in the same tick narrow the {{ vm.barracks_spy_error_percent }}% fuzz, because each one puts the true number in its own range and the ranges overlap. Conf tells you how tight that got: locked means they pinned it exactly, otherwise you get the margin that's left.

5. Why your number can differ

In rough order of how often it's the answer.

  1. Units owned versus units at home. The main OP figure counts every unit the dominion owns, including any out on an invasion right now. A tool showing what's home this tick will be lower, and both of you are right. The Cur OP column is the home-only view.
  2. The training queue. The game's own Offense Calculator assumes a dominion keeps training at the same rate, so it counts the full queue. OD Info counts what's actually left and drops the ticks that already landed. Against a dominion training hard, OD Info reads lower than the game. That's deliberate on the game's side.
  3. Spells that may not be up. Every race gets credited with its self-spell. On a dominion that's asleep, out of mana, or just out of protection, that's up to 10% of OP that isn't there.
  4. Ops you're missing. No Vision, no tech bonus. No Castle Spy, no forges. No Survey Dominion, no gryphon nests. Each one quietly counts as zero, so thin intel always reads low. Check Ops Age before you trust a number.
  5. Barracks Spy fuzz. Every number on one is off by up to {{ vm.barracks_spy_error_percent }}% either way. When the Barracks Spy is the freshest op, that error rides straight into the OP. A second one in the same tick genuinely narrows it.
  6. Ops taken at different times. The bonuses come from up to four separate ops. If your Survey is a day older than your Clear Sight, you're looking at yesterday's buildings on today's army.
  7. Buildings under construction. Counted as though they're finished, as above. A dominion mid-rebuild reads slightly ahead of what the game shows it.
  8. Clear Sight isn't always exact. A realm holding the Spire of Illusion has its Clear Sight results fuzzed, and the tool takes Clear Sight at face value.

6. What the tool doesn't count yet

Each one skews the estimate in a predictable direction. Poke AgFx if you want one of them fixed soon.

  • Wonders. Nothing realm-wide from wonders is counted. A realm holding the Obelisk of Power or the Lair of the Dragon hits harder than shown, and one behind the Great Wall defends better.
  • Units that ignore draftees. Draftees always count towards DP here, so a dominion facing a Dark Elf or Spirit attack looks better defended than it is.
  • Offense that varies with land range. A Gnome Juggernaut and a classic Dark Elf Spirit Warrior hit harder or softer depending on the size of the target. They're counted at base value, so Gnome OP in particular reads low.
  • Offense and defense from buildings on the classic Dark Elf Adept, and summoned units on Planewalker.
