What OD Info is for

OD Info is a planning tool. It's built around one question: what will the round look like {{ vm.build_ticks }} ticks from now?

The {{ vm.build_ticks }} tick clock

Almost everything you build takes {{ vm.build_ticks }} ticks to finish. That single fact sets your horizon.

What you start now is what you'll have half a day from now. So you need to know today who'll be able to reach you then, and how much defence you'd better be holding when they do.

Every number in here points at that question. It's also why some of them won't match what the game shows you this second.

Why everything counts as paid

The tool counts what a dominion has committed to. Units in the training queue count. Buildings under construction count. Land that's already on its way counts. At a {{ vm.build_ticks }} tick horizon all of it is real, and that's the horizon you're planning against.

So a figure here can read higher than what the game shows that dominion right now. That's the intended answer. The Paid column tells you how many ticks are left before their queue lands.

Want the right-now view instead? The Current Strength columns on the Military page give you that.

What it won't do

It won't tell you whether to send. The numbers rest on assumptions that are fine for planning and shaky for a final call: spells assumed up, barracks spy figures carrying their fuzz, bonuses pulled from ops taken hours apart. Any of those can be off on the dominion in front of you.

Use it to work out who's worth a look and what you need ready. Then take fresh ops and check the target in the game's own calculator before you commit. How OP and DP are calculated goes through where the two drift apart.

Why it keeps history

Every update adds a timestamped copy instead of overwriting the last one. One op tells you where a dominion stands. A run of them tells you where it's heading, which is what you actually need {{ vm.build_ticks }} ticks out.

That's also why the age of an op sits on every page, colour coded. Over an hour old, look twice. Past {{ vm.build_ticks }} ticks, refresh it before you trust it.

The tool never plays for you. It takes no actions in the game and collects nothing on its own, because that's against the rules. You play, you take the ops, you pull the results in here.

What each section is for

Overview
Every dominion in the round with its land, its networth, and how far that networth moved in the last 12 hours. Where you start, and where you keep coming back to.
Town Crier
A copy of the game's event feed, wiped and refreshed every time you update it. There's little reason to read it here, since the game shows you the same events. It's in the app because it's raw material: Award Stats runs entirely off it, and there's more to be squeezed out of who has been hitting whom than the tool does today.
Networth Tracker
Two questions on one page. The top of the list is who's growing fastest, which is a threat forming. The Unchanged NW list below it is the opposite: dominions whose networth hasn't budged, which usually means nobody's home. An inactive dominion has probably let Ares Call drop, and that makes it a target.
Ratios
Wizard and spy ratios per dominion. Whether your ops will land on them, and whether theirs will land on you.
Military Tracker
The planning board. What each dominion could send, what it'd have left at home, and what your own defence has to beat once their temples are counted.
Realmies
Your own realm, seen the way an enemy scanning it sees it. Who's under-defended, who's missing Ares Call, who has no boats to send with. Find your soft spots before somebody else finds them for you.
Stealables
Where the resources are sitting, with your own realm and the bots filtered out.
Award Stats
Pure fun, this one. Bounce counts and war declarations tallied out of the Town Crier events, for handing out awards to the realm when the round wraps up.
{% if 'economy' in feature_toggles %}
Economy
Your own dominion's jobs and platinum production. Off unless you switch it on in secret.txt.
