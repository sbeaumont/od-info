How the Threats tab works

The Threats tab answers one question for one realmie: who can hit this dominion in the next {{ vm.horizon }} ticks, at which tick, and what has to happen first?

Most attackers do not send their full OP, because a full send leaves them weak at home. Other dominions hold them back. When a top OP sends, it stops holding the others back, and many hits follow in the next ticks. The tab shows those chains, tick by tick.

Terms

Defender
The realmie you pick in the dropdown.
Attacker
A dominion in another realm that has the defender in range.
Threat
A dominion that can hit the attacker back while the army of the attacker is out.
5/4 send
The most OP a dominion can send under the 5/4 rule.
Safe send
The most OP a dominion can send while its home DP still beats every threat to it.
Trigger
A threat that has to send first, before the safe send of the attacker breaks the defender.
Free sender
A dominion whose home DP after a 5/4 send beats every threat to it.

Range

An attacker can hit the defender at a tick when the land of the defender is between {{ vm.min_range }}% and {{ vm.max_range }}% of the land of the attacker at that tick. A hit below {{ vm.min_range }}% is bottom feeding. The game penalizes it heavily, so it happens only as a last measure near the end of the round. You can set a lower minimum range on the page to see those attackers too.

Land changes during the {{ vm.horizon }} ticks. An invasion in the Town Crier gives the attacker {{ vm.conquest_land_factor }} times the land its target lost, {{ vm.land_arrival_ticks }} ticks after the hit. A Land Spy also shows land from exploring. It replaces the invasions from before its time, and invasions after it add to it. A daily land bonus gives {{ vm.daily_land_bonus }} acres at once. Tick the box on the page to see what claiming it at a given tick does to your range.

OP and DP at a tick

For each tick from now to {{ vm.horizon }} ticks ahead, the tab takes the military that is home at that tick:

  • Units at home now stay home.
  • Returning units count from the tick they arrive.
  • Units in training count from the tick their training completes.

A hit happens at one tick, so the military at that tick is what counts. Units that are out on an attack do not count in the OP of a dominion until they return. Your realmies have exact numbers from the realm advisors. The numbers of other dominions come from their last ops.

The safe send of an attacker

  1. Find all threats to the attacker at the tick: dominions in another realm that have the attacker in range.
  2. Calculate the 5/4 send of each threat, with its temples, against the DP bonus of the attacker.
  3. Take the strongest one. The attacker has to keep enough DP home to beat it.
  4. Send the units that are free to go: pure offense units first, then hybrid units from best OP/DP ratio down, until the DP that stays home is just enough.
  5. The OP that goes out is the safe send. It is never more than the 5/4 send.

A free sender has a safe send equal to its 5/4 send.

Can the attacker break the defender?

Take the safe send of the attacker with its temples, against the DP bonus of the defender. Compare it with the DP of the defender at the tick. The difference is the margin. A positive margin means that the attacker can break the defender without risk.

Triggers: what sets an attacker free

When a strong dominion sends, its army is out until it returns. During that time it is no threat to anyone. Every dominion that it held back can send more.

If the safe send does not break the defender, the tab looks for the trigger:

  1. Put the threats to the attacker in order, strongest first.
  2. Take away the strongest threat. Calculate the safe send again without it.
  3. If the safe send now breaks the defender, stop. The threats taken away are the triggers.
  4. If not, take away the next threat and go back to step 2.
  5. Stop after {{ vm.max_triggers }} triggers. A longer chain depends on old ops of many dominions, so the tab does not show it.
  6. If the attacker still cannot break the defender, the attacker has no safe hit at this tick.

The defender can itself be a threat to the attacker. When the tab takes the defender away, the defender has sent. It then keeps only its home DP after a full 5/4 send, and the attacker has to beat that DP instead of the full DP. So a defender that sends can make itself a target, even for an attacker that cannot break its full DP.

For each trigger, the tab also shows if the trigger is itself a free sender. A free trigger can send at any time. A trigger that is held back needs its own trigger first, so that chain is less likely.

Planning a send: Home DP

Before you send, enter the DP that stays at home in the Home DP box. The game shows it on the invade page. The tab then uses that DP for the defender at every tick. Training and returning units still add DP at the later ticks, the same amount as without the send. The army of the defender is out, so the defender is no threat and no trigger for any attacker. Leave the box empty to see the defender with its army at home.

The two tables

Out of range holds the dominions that have the defender below their minimum range at every tick. These are the larger dominions that the defender can grow into range of: from a hit of its own, from exploring or from a daily land bonus. A hit can make the defender jump far, so the table holds them at any distance. The cells show what the dominion can do if the defender were in its range at that tick.

In range holds every attacker that has the defender in range at one or more of the {{ vm.horizon }} ticks.

Both tables start with the largest {{ vm.min_range }}% limit. So the page goes from the largest dominion at the top, through the ones just above your range, down to the smallest attacker.

Both tables show a dominion only when it is a threat: one or more cells are red, orange, yellow or a potential threat. A potential threat is a 5/4 send that does not break the defender, but comes within {{ vm.potential_margin }}% of the DP of the defender. Tick "Show all" to see the other dominions too.

The {{ vm.min_range }}% column of both tables shows the smallest land that the dominion has in range now. The tooltip of each cell shows that limit at the tick, and how many acres the defender is above it (+) or below it (−). In the Out of range table, this tells you how many acres you can still grow. In the In range table, it tells you how far you are from getting out of range.

Reading the grid

Dark greyemptyThe attacker does not have the defender in range at this tick.

The value is the OP at home minus the DP of the defender, in thousands. Units that are out on an attack or still in training do not count until they arrive. So a strong attacker with its army out can show a large negative number. The tooltip then says "army out", with the tick at which the last returning units arrive.

The army out comes from the returning units of the last BS. When the Town Crier shows a hit or bounce by the attacker after its last BS, the tab does not know which units went out. The row is dim, and the tooltip asks you to take a BS. The tooltip also shows how exact the numbers are: "locked" for exact numbers, or the fuzz that is left, for example "±8%".

A row with ops older than {{ vm.horizon }} hours is dim.

What the tab leaves out

  • The threat to an attacker counts as the full 5/4 send of that threat. A threat that is itself held back cannot send its full OP. So the safe send is the lowest value, and a real attacker can send somewhat more.
  • An attacker can accept risk and send more than its safe send. The yellow cells show where a full 5/4 send breaks the defender.
  • The tab shows one hit at a time. A realm can hit one target with several dominions in a row.
  • Draftees stay at their last count. They grow between trainings in the game.
  • Land from exploring is known only from a Land Spy.
  • Other dominions can claim a daily land bonus too. The tab does not know when.
  • Guards limit the range in both directions. The tab uses the normal range only.
